Our philosophy

Educating the whole child.

“We strive to help all children develop their unique physical, emotional, intellectual and spiritual capacities. This enables each individual to responsibly enter in freedom into an ever-changing local and global community.”

In Waldorf there is no one-size-fits-all approach to learning. Our core guiding principle is a deep respect for the individual. Children move through ideas with their whole selves: they think it, feel it and make it.

The journey

From first steps to Class 7,
one unhurried path.

Like growth rings in a tree, every stage builds on the last. Nothing is rushed, and everything is woven together.

  1. 18 months – 6 years

    Kindergarten

    Playgroup & Kinder classes

    A sensory haven of calming colours, enticing scents and natural materials, where play is the work.

    • Storytelling
    • Songs & plays
    • Free play
    • Cooking & baking
    • Watercolour
    • Beeswax modelling
    • Finger knitting
    • Nature walks
  2. Classes 1 – 3

    Lower Primary

    Learning through story & rhythm

    Letters, numbers and the natural world arrive through stories, movement, drama and painting before they reach the page.

    • Main-lesson blocks
    • Expressive movement
    • Drama & poetry
    • Recorder
    • Farming block

    🌾 Class 3 visits a farm after the Farming block to experience farm life first-hand.

  3. Classes 4 – 7

    Upper Primary

    Reason meets wonder

    Sciences, history and geography deepen, while woodwork, sculpture and music keep thinking connected to doing.

    • Sciences
    • History & Geography
    • Woodwork
    • Sculpture
    • Instruments
    • Athletics

    ⛰️ Class 4 visits the Ngong Hills after the Local Geography block.

Ask us anything
What happens after Class 7?

Waldorf Woodlands teaches up to Class 7. To confirm: where our graduates usually continue, and how we support the move into Junior Secondary.

How does Waldorf education fit with Kenya’s national curriculum?

Our primary curriculum covers English, French, Swahili, Mathematics, Sciences, Geography, History, Art, Music, Woodwork, Swimming, and Sports & Games. Each is taught through Waldorf methods: story, movement, art and hands-on work.

To confirm: how the curriculum maps to the national framework, and how children take part in national assessments.

When will my child learn to read and write?

In Waldorf schools, formal reading and writing usually begin in Class 1, at around 6 to 7 years old. Before that, Kindergarten builds the foundations through storytelling, songs, rhymes and play. Letters are often introduced through pictures and stories, so they carry meaning from the start.

To confirm: how we track and share each child’s literacy progress.

How is progress assessed without regular exams?

Waldorf schools generally favour close observation and detailed written reports over frequent testing. Class teachers often stay with the same children for several years and come to know each one deeply.

To confirm: how often reports and parent–teacher meetings happen, and any formal assessments used.

What is your approach to screens and technology?

Waldorf education traditionally keeps screens out of the early years, favouring real-world, hands-on experience. Technology is introduced later, once children have strong foundations in thinking, imagination and making.
